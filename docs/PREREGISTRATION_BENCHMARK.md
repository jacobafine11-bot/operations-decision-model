# Pre-registration: the multi-company benchmark

**Written and committed before any benchmark company was generated or
simulated.** Purpose: show whether the whole pipeline (history → validation
gate → estimates → predictions → decision) works across many different
operations, not just one.

## Companies

80 made-up companies, generated by `analysis/benchmark.py` from fixed
seeds, in two size bands:

| Band | Total workers | Stations in the line |
|---|---|---|
| S | 2 to 100 | 2 to 6 |
| L | 100 to 250 | 3 to 7 |

Each band: companies 0 to 19 are the **development set** (results may be
inspected and the model may be fixed, with a mechanism and a changelog
entry); companies 20 to 39 are the **sealed set**, run exactly once after
the model is frozen. No sealed company is generated, printed, or
simulated before the freeze commit.

How a company is drawn (all uniform unless stated): total workers in the
band; stations; workers split across stations at random (at least 1
each); one bottleneck station at utilization 0.80 to 0.93, the others
0.50 to 0.90; work-time variability cv 0.3 to 1.5 per station; orders
arrive at random (Poisson) at about 1.5 to 4 orders per hour per worker.
Costs: waiting cost $2 to $15 per order-hour; loaded wage $22 to $38 per
hour; operating hours 2,000, 4,000, or 6,000 per year.

Four candidate changes per company, so 16 possible plans:

1. Process change at the bottleneck: +10% to +30% speed, small one-time cost.
2. Automation at the bottleneck: +40% to +90% speed, large one-time cost
   plus 5% of it per year.
3. Hire at the bottleneck: about 10% more workers there (at least 1),
   costing wage × hours each year.
4. Second-busiest station: either a speed change or a hire (coin flip).

Budget for one-time costs: 0.5 to 1.5 times the automation price, so
automation is sometimes unaffordable.

## What the model gets (exactly what a real engagement would have)

A 30,000-order history from the company's TRUE operation (independent
simulator, millisecond timestamps), run through the validation gate and
the DuckDB estimation step. Crew sizes, costs, and candidate changes come
"from the owner" (the true values). The model never sees the true work
speeds, variability, or arrival rate.

## What counts as the truth

- Waiting and total time: a separate 100,000-order run of the true
  operation (different seed), warmup 10% discarded.
- The true value of every plan: the true operation re-simulated before
  and after the plan, 4 replications of 20,000 orders each with common
  random numbers, gross yearly savings = waiting cost × arrival rate ×
  drop in mean total time × hours per year, minus the plan's annualized
  cost. "Do nothing" is worth exactly 0.

## Scorecard (each band separately, sealed set only)

| ID | Criterion | Pass if |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Median absolute error, predicted vs true total time through the line | ≤ 10% |
| P2 | Companies with total-time error within ±20% | ≥ 90% |
| P3 | Median absolute error of station waits (stations whose true wait ≥ 1 minute) | ≤ 15% |
| D1 | Companies where the model's chosen plan is near-best: true value within max(10% of the best plan's true value, $2,000/yr) of the best | ≥ 80% |
| D2 | Median regret (true best minus true value of the model's choice), as a share of the best plan's value | ≤ 5% |
| D3 | Companies where the model's choice does not lose money (true value ≥ −$2,000/yr) | ≥ 95% |
| D4 | Median absolute error of promised vs truly delivered gross savings, for the model's chosen plan (when it isn't "do nothing") | ≤ 15% |

Why these: P1 to P3 check the predictions a manager would see; D1 to D4
check the thing that matters, whether acting on the model's advice is
nearly as good as knowing the truth. The $2,000/yr floor in D1 and D3
keeps simulation noise on near-tied plans from counting as a model error.

## Amendment 1 (2026-11-19), committed before either sealed set was run

**What the development set showed about the TEST (not the model).**
1. The decisions were too predictable. In all 40 development companies
   the best plan contained a cheap process change at the bottleneck;
   "do nothing" and "hire" never won. A rule of thumb ("fix the
   bottleneck cheaply") would have scored well, so D1 to D4 could not
   distinguish a good model from a lucky heuristic.
2. P3 is undefined in band L: no station had a true wait of 1 minute or
   more (median 2.4 seconds), because large crews rarely make anyone wait.

**Changes.**
- The original sealed set (companies 20 to 39 per band, original
  generator) is run unchanged, exactly as pre-registered above.
- A second sealed set, **"hard decisions"** (companies 20 to 39 per band,
  generator variant `hard`), is added and scored with the same P1 to D4
  thresholds. The variant widens the ranges so that every kind of answer
  can win: waiting cost $0.50 to $40 per order-hour (log-uniform);
  process change +2% to +25% speed at $2,000 to $60,000 × size
  (log-uniform); automation +20% to +100% at $20,000 to $400,000 × size
  (log-uniform); loaded wage $15 to $38; bottleneck utilization 0.75 to
  0.95; other stations 0.30 to 0.92. Before its sealed run, 10 hard
  development companies per band (0 to 9) are run only to confirm the
  variant produces a real mix of right answers (do nothing, hiring, and
  automation each winning somewhere); the model may not be changed on
  the basis of them.
- P3 when no station qualifies: reported as "not applicable" and counted
  as neither pass nor fail. For information only (not gated), P3b
  reports the same median error for stations with true wait ≥ 0.1 minute.
- An extra diagnostic is reported, not gated: how often the model picks
  the exact best plan, the number of close calls (runner-up within 10%
  of the best), and how often a fixed rule of thumb ("process change at
  the bottleneck, alone", the most common best plan in the development
  set) would have been near-best. This shows whether the
  decision tests can tell skill from luck.

**Model status.** No model change was needed on the development set
(band S: 7/7; band L: 6/6 defined criteria). The model is frozen at the
commit containing this amendment.

## Rules for the development set

Fixes are allowed only with a mechanism, a changelog entry, and the
verification suite still passing. Thresholds above may not be changed.
After the freeze commit the sealed set runs once; its result is reported
as is.
